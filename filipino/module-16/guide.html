For the adult reading partner

Dorothy at ang Munting Parada

A concrete, cheerful story about order, rhythm, a small mishap, and working together—with no science concept that must be mastered before the ending makes sense.

What Module 15 established

RJ read three new pages without interruption and then, when invited to retell them in English, gave an almost line-by-line translation. That is strong evidence that a flowing first reading preserves both the main mental picture and the details. Routine line-by-line translation is no longer needed. It remains available as a diagnostic tool when her retelling, the picture, and the text appear to disagree.

Three new pages also turned out not to be an externally imposed limit: RJ herself prefers three, followed by nine to twelve familiar pages chosen with cards and one printed question for each. That voluntary gamification is productive rereading, not a distraction from it. This guide therefore preserves her chosen structure.

A five-session rhythm chosen by RJ

  1. Sessions 1–4: read three new pages aloud without stopping. Then ask for one brief retell in English or Filipino. If the account is accurate, move on.
  2. After the new pages: use the card deck for familiar pages as long as RJ wants it. The card value chooses the page and the suit may choose one of the four questions.
  3. Translation: sample one line only when there is a real reason—an unclear event, an interesting expression, or a mismatch in the mental picture.
  4. Session 5: revisit favorite pages, reconstruct the whole story, or discuss the six end questions. This is consolidation, not a late finish.
  5. Thirty minutes remains a ceiling: stop while the story and game are still enjoyable. Missing school-exam days requires no compensation.

The page questions remain extensive because they supply trustworthy Filipino to the adult. They are a deck of choices, not a worksheet.

At a glance

  • 12 pages and exactly 265 running Filipino words
  • 7 original watercolor-and-colored-pencil illustrations
  • 6 optional end questions and 48 optional page questions
  • 32 programmatically new story surface forms beyond the TSV and Modules 0–15; many are transparent forms of familiar families, so this is an exposure count rather than a mastery target
  • A visible sequence: arrange the line, fail by rushing, follow a drumbeat, stop for tangled ribbons, and complete the parade together
  • Return of Dorothy, Toto, Tik-Tok, Polychrome, the yellow bird, and Ozma

English translation

Page 1

One morning, Ozma planned a little parade in the garden. “We will begin when the bell rings,” she said. The friends prepared at once.

Page 2

Dorothy arranged their line. Toto was at the front. Dorothy, Tik-Tok, and Polychrome followed. The yellow bird would fly above them.

Page 3

Dorothy was holding a blue flag. Polychrome had long ribbons. Tik-Tok had a little drum. The bird would wear a red hat and sing.

Page 4

They began to practice. Suddenly Toto ran very fast. He reached the rose arch. The friends were left behind, and their line broke apart.

Page 5

Toto returned when Dorothy called him. “We do not need to be fast,” she said. “We need to walk together.”

Page 6

Tik-Tok played: tok, tok, tok. They walked with every beat. Toto was still at the front, but he was no longer running.

Page 7

Suddenly the wind blew. Polychrome’s long ribbons wound around a branch. The whole line stopped at once.

Page 8

Polychrome held the ends. Dorothy slowly loosened the ribbons from the branch. They were not torn.

Page 9

They continued practicing around the fountain. The bird sang. They turned together while Toto listened to the drum.

Page 10

The bell rang. “The parade is about to begin!” said Ozma. The visitors waited on both sides of the path.

Page 11

Toto led. Behind him came Dorothy with the flag, Tik-Tok with the drum, and Polychrome with colorful ribbons. The bird flew and sang.

Page 12

When they returned to Ozma, the friends bowed. “You were not the fastest,” she said, “but you observed, listened, and helped one another.” They were all happy.

Short answers to the six end questions

  1. Kapag tumunog ang kampana. (Page 1)
  2. May bandila si Dorothy, may tambol si Tik-Tok, may mga laso si Polychrome, nangunguna si Toto, at lumilipad at umaawit ang ibon. (Pages 2–3 and 11)
  3. Tumakbo nang mabilis si Toto at naiwan ang mga kaibigan. (Page 4)
  4. Tumugtog siya ng tambol, at lumakad sila sa bawat kumpas. (Page 6)
  5. Pumulupot ang mga laso sa sanga. Hinawakan ni Polychrome ang mga dulo at dahan-dahang kinalas ni Dorothy ang mga laso. (Pages 7–8)
  6. Pinuri niya ang kanilang pagmamasid, pakikinig, at pagtutulungan. (Page 12)

What to observe before Module 17

Continue with informal observations rather than a score:

  • Does the ordinary parade story remain satisfying without a science problem?
  • Can RJ name each participant’s place, object, or action in the parade?
  • Can she explain why Toto’s first fast run breaks the line and why Tik-Tok’s beat helps?
  • Can she retell the ribbon problem and the two-person solution?
  • Do magsisimula/nagsimula, lilipad/lumipad, and tumatakbo/tumakbo remain clear from context without a grammar lesson?
  • Does uninterrupted three-page reading again produce an accurate gist or detailed retell?
  • Does an occasional translation sample reveal anything that the retell missed?
  • Does RJ still choose three new pages, or does she ever ask to continue?
  • Does the card-and-suit review remain enjoyable across nine to twelve familiar pages?
  • Does the optional fifth session make the end questions more approachable?
  • Which page questions most readily produce Filipino answers?
  • What practical unfamiliar-word count results, including older-but-unsettled forms?
  • Are the line order, drumbeat, tangled ribbons, and final parade clear in the pictures?
  • Which harmless visual differences reward close reading, and which illustration becomes her favorite?
  • Do the stress support, thirty-minute ceiling, rhythm, and enjoyment remain strong?

A useful short report: note story comprehension; parade order and roles; first failed attempt; drumbeat and ribbon solution; contextual paired forms; response to uninterrupted reading and selective translation; chosen pages per session; card review; fifth-day questions; Filipino answers; practical unfamiliar count; text-art clarity and productive discrepancies; pronunciation; rhythm; and enjoyment.

Production note

This is an original educational story inspired by the public-domain literary world of Oz. The yellow bird in the red floppy hat is an original recurring friend created for this project. The prose, questions, and original watercolor-and-colored-pencil illustrations were created with OpenAI tools and human review for this reader. They avoid film-specific likenesses and embedded text.
