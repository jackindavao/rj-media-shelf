For the adult reading with RJ

Dorothy at si Propesor Wogglebug at ang Susing Bakal

This guide adds three scrupulously written Filipino questions and model answers for every page. Choose whichever question suits the moment; the page reading should still feel like a conversation, not a quiz.

Calibration decision

Module 7 remained exactly at the right level. RJ remembered all three color mixtures, retold each character's contribution easily, connected the paired verb forms in context, used some short Filipino answers voluntarily, and enjoyed Polychrome's continued presence. The adult counted about 29 unfamiliar forms, including transparent relatives of known words such as aalis beside familiar umalis.

The new page-by-page routine is valuable: RJ reads one page, then answers a question about that page in Filipino when she can and in English whenever she wishes. Because the adult should not have to invent “Tarzan Filipino,” this guide supplies three natural questions and exact answer models for each of the 12 pages.

RJ's discovery of illustration changes is also valuable. Future art should preserve facts needed to understand the story and science, but harmless differences in secondary details may remain available for critical comparison. Asking “Does the picture say exactly the same thing as the words?” is now an intended close-reading opportunity, not a hunt for mistakes.

Why Professor Wogglebug fits here

RJ asked to meet Professor Wogglebug. Baum's original Woggle-Bug is an ordinary insect made human-sized, proud of being “Highly Magnified” and “Thoroughly Educated.” A related Baum text describes his delight in brilliant red, yellow, blue, and green clothing. This module keeps the recognizable large, formally dressed insect scholar while giving him a warm, useful role suited to RJ's Oz.

His recurring design begins here: a tall, friendly, upright beetle-like scholar with two legs, four hands, two antennae, gentle spectacles, and a brilliant patchwork formal coat in red, yellow, blue, and green. He carries a small satchel and bows politely. The design is original to this series and does not copy a screen portrayal.

The small science idea

A magnet attracts some metals, including iron and many kinds of steel, but it does not attract every metal. The Smithsonian Science Education Center's Grade 3 physical-science guidance explicitly treats “magnets attract certain metals, including iron, but not all metals” as the investigation pattern. The University of Wisconsin–Madison's magnetism explainer specifically notes that copper is not attracted to ordinary magnets.

The story therefore tests wood and stone, confirms the magnet with an explicitly iron spoon, and then uses Tik-Tok's copper hand to challenge the tempting but incorrect rule that every metal will stick. The lost iron key supplies a practical reason to perform the same test in the grass.

Optional real-world test: If you try this after reading, use a child-safe magnet with adult supervision and clearly identified objects. Keep strong magnets away from electronics, cards, and very young children. Real spoons differ: use an iron or magnet-attracting steel object rather than assuming every shiny spoon will stick.

Story map

  1. Polychrome remains, and the brightly dressed Professor Wogglebug arrives with a gift for Ozma.
  2. The tiny iron key to his green box falls into grass and disappears beneath leaves.
  3. Dorothy and Polychrome search but cannot see it.
  4. The professor produces a magnet and explains that iron sticks but not every metal does.
  5. Wood and stone fail the test; an iron spoon sticks; Tik-Tok's copper hand does not.
  6. The friends pass the magnet over the grass until it catches the hidden key.
  7. The opened box contains a book about stars for Ozma, and Polychrome asks for another experiment.

Using the page-by-page questions

  1. After RJ reads a page, choose one of its three questions. The extras are alternatives for later rereadings, not a required set.
  2. Ask the printed Filipino exactly as written. English answers, short Filipino phrases, pointing, or a mixed answer all count if the meaning is clear.
  3. If needed, reread the relevant line and offer the model answer once. No grammar explanation or repetition is required.
  4. On another reading, choose a different question. Over four days the same page can support several fresh, predictable conversations.

Pronunciation key

The accent marks below indicate the strongest syllable; a grave or circumflex accent also signals a final glottal stop. Keep the marks in this adult guide, not in RJ's story.

Quick key: propesór, batubalanì, bákal, susì, insékto, regálo, tansô, dumikít, dumidikít, kumápit, yumukô.

For Wogglebug, a practical English reading is WOG-uhl-bug. As always, the nephew's fluent recording should outrank any written approximation.

Grammar and patterns that matter

1. Dumidikit becomes dumikit

The professor first states a general pattern with dumidikit. Each test then reports whether something dumikit. The repeated pictures and materials carry the timing and meaning; no conjugation lesson is needed.

2. Material words link the tests

Bakal, metal, and tanso let the story refine its rule. Tik-Tok is metal, but his copper hand does not stick. The adult can simply reread his sentence rather than explain categories abstractly.

3. Question words stay stable

The page bank repeatedly uses sino, ano, saan, bakit, and paano. Familiar question openings should make it easier for RJ to predict what kind of answer is wanted.

4. Short phrases are complete answers

Sa damo, ang susi, wala pa, and dahan-dahan all answer their questions naturally. A full sentence may grow later from repeated exposure; it is not required now.

Production note

This is an original educational story using characters from L. Frank Baum's literary Oz world. Professor Wogglebug's broad literary identity is retained, while his warm role, recurring visual design, story, and watercolor-and-colored-pencil illustrations were created specifically for this reader. They intentionally avoid film- and television-specific likenesses. This project is not affiliated with any Oz screen production.
