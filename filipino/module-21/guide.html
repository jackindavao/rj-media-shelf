For the adult reading partner

Dorothy at ang Munting Tunog sa Palasyo

A soft sound leads Dorothy and her friends through Ozma’s palace. Trot and both birds help follow the clues.

How to read this one

This is an ordinary adventure with no procedure or science concept to teach. Do a quick VocDog preview if wanted, then read a page or a few pages aloud. If RJ chooses to review the current story first, that works too. One card question for a familiar page is plenty; leave room for the rabbit trails she asks about.

Short Filipino answers, quoted phrases, pointing, and English retells all count. Use a question to check a detail if it matters to the story. The end questions can wait for another day.

12 pages · 265 running Filipino words · 7 illustrations · 48 page questions · 6 end questions.

English translation

Page 1

Dorothy tied her hair with a blue ribbon. She entered Ozma’s palace with Trot and Captain Bill.

Page 2

Ozma welcomed them in the great hall. The two birds perched beside the window. Suddenly they heard a soft sound.

Page 3

“Is someone playing music?” asked Trot. “Let’s look,” said Captain Bill. They walked along the long hallway. Ozma went with them.

Page 4

In the first room there was a big harp beside the window. Nobody was playing. Trot went up to the harp, but it was silent.

Page 5

They heard the sound again. “It sounds like something is rolling,” said Captain Bill. “Over there.” The yellow bird flew to the next room.

Page 6

Dorothy and Trot entered a room full of books. They looked under the table. They found nothing there.

Page 7

The yellow bird perched beside the open door. Dorothy heard the sound coming from there. The two children entered.

Page 8

Inside, the red bird saw the blue curtain move. The bird flew down to the floor. Trot pointed to the little tail.

Page 9

Trot gently drew the curtain aside. Ozma’s pink kitten was there. The kitten pushed the little golden ball.

Page 10

The ball rolled across the floor. They heard the sound. “So that was it!” said Dorothy. She picked up the ball and the kitten came near.

Page 11

They went back to the hall. The kitten followed Dorothy. “Here’s the ball,” said Dorothy. She set it beside Ozma’s chair.

Page 12

The kitten jumped onto Ozma’s lap. The two birds perched by the window again. Now the whole hall was quiet.

What to observe before Module 22

Informal observations, not a score:

  • Which room would RJ want to visit? Did she like Dorothy’s ponytail?
  • Can she trace the sound from the hall to the curtain, and tell what made it?
  • Is the tail visible as a clue before the kitten appears? Do the two birds’ roles stay distinct?
  • Do gumugulong and gumulong make sense in context?
  • How did the vocabulary load compare with the knot story, including question words and rusty forms?
  • Did one-page reading, the brief preview, and card review support an easy retell? Was there any question wording you had to untangle?
