For the adult reading partner

Dorothy at ang Paanyaya kay Scarecrow

The two bird friends meet Scarecrow in a cornfield, overcome an understandable hesitation, and use their view from above to recover Ozma's windblown invitation.

What Module 18 established

RJ enthusiastically accepted the red bird as the yellow bird's continuing companion and welcomed the pink kitten. She understood all four book categories, the picture markers, the misleading cat cover, and the rule of looking inside before choosing a section. Her Filipino answers are increasingly original, uninterrupted three-page reading still produces a detailed retell, and she continues to choose three new pages followed by card review.

The practical unfamiliar count was about 45, including older-but-unsettled forms. That was manageable, but it is evidence to keep the conceptual cluster small. The story therefore returns to one concrete place, one social hesitation, and one easily pictured search problem. The end questions retain one or two causal prompts without making every question causal.

RJ also gave two pieces of story direction: the red bird should stay with the yellow bird, and Ozma has worn the same dress long enough. Both requests are honored here. Ozma keeps her dark hair, emerald circlet, and green slippers, but wears a new sapphire-blue, ivory, and rose dress.

The brief VocDog preview

Your ten-word preview is a sound compromise between pure “osmosis” and stopping to translate during the story. It removes avoidable decoding friction while leaving the story, pictures, repetition, and context to do the lasting teaching.

  • Keep it brief: two to four minutes is enough. Recognition today is the goal; durable recall before reading is not.
  • Preview only likely blockers: include new items and a few older items whose absence would hide the scene. Do not turn every unfamiliar form into a card.
  • Use phrases when the phrase carries the picture: taniman ng mais, bugso ng hangin, and mula sa itaas are more useful than isolated pieces.
  • Let the story finish the job: if one item remains uncertain after a quick cycle, begin reading. Meeting it in a meaningful sentence is part of the method.
  • Treat removal as “ready for this page,” not “mastered”: future stories and card review supply the spacing and repetition.

If a preview regularly exceeds about ten items or begins to feel like the main event, reduce the next story's novelty rather than lengthening the drill.

English translation

Page 1

One morning, Ozma prepared an invitation for Scarecrow. She had a new blue-and-white dress.

Page 2

Ozma tied the invitation with a blue ribbon. “Please deliver this to Scarecrow,” she said to Dorothy and the two birds.

Page 3

Dorothy walked on the yellow path. The yellow bird and red bird flew beside her. They reached the cornfield.

Page 4

Scarecrow was in the middle of the field. The two birds stopped on a fence. “Scarecrows frighten birds,” said the red bird.

Page 5

“This friend of ours is kind,” Dorothy answered. The yellow bird approached first. When Scarecrow smiled, the red bird followed.

Page 6

Scarecrow bowed to the visitors. He read the invitation. “I will be happy to come,” he said.

Page 7

Suddenly, a gust of wind arrived. It carried the invitation out of Scarecrow's hand. The white paper flew above the corn.

Page 8

Dorothy ran between the rows. She saw the blue ribbon caught on a leaf. But the invitation was not there.

Page 9

“We will look from above,” said the yellow bird. The bird friends soared. Between two rows, they spotted the paper.

Page 10

The red bird flew down and went beside the invitation. The yellow bird called Dorothy. Dorothy followed the voice and found the paper.

Page 11

Scarecrow tucked the invitation inside his hat. “It is safe now,” he said. Together they walked back to the palace.

Page 12

Ozma welcomed them in the garden. Scarecrow sat beside the book cart. While Dorothy read, the two birds perched on Scarecrow's shoulders.

Production note

This is an original educational story inspired by the public-domain literary world of Oz. The yellow bird in the red floppy hat and the red bird in the yellow floppy hat are original recurring friends created for this project. The prose, questions, and original watercolor-and-colored-pencil illustrations were created with OpenAI tools and human review. They avoid film-specific likenesses, embedded text, and watermarks.
