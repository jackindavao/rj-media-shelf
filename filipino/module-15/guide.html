For the adult reading partner

Dorothy at ang Tatlong Nawawalang Pahina

This module is deliberately story-first. Reading with understanding is the activity; translation and questions are tools to use only when they help.

What changes after Module 14

RJ understood the visible ice sequence and continued to enjoy the reading, but the controlled-comparison and insulation questions required scientific ideas beyond the story’s language level. That is useful calibration evidence. Module 15 returns to an ordinary narrative problem: three picture-book pages blow away, the friends retrieve them, order the pictured events, and finish a gift for Ozma. Every end question can be answered from the story itself.

The question banks remain because they give the adult reliable Filipino. They are a menu, not a checklist. Asking none is entirely acceptable. A question has earned its place only when it supports attention, conversation, or fun.

Reading for meaning, without becoming too “teacher-y”

The proposed shift is sound: exact translation of every line is no longer the best default. Extensive-reading research finds overall benefits for reading proficiency, while vocabulary-coverage research warns that genuinely independent reading is usually easier at much higher coverage than “eight or nine known words out of ten.” The important distinction here is that 25 unfamiliar types or forms in a 265-word story does not mean that 10 percent of its running words are unknown. Some forms repeat, some belong to familiar families, and RJ also has pictures, an adult partner, prior knowledge, and rereading.

This is therefore supported reading rather than a test of unassisted reading. Her observed ability to follow the plot, explain details, laugh at discrepancies, and anticipate patterns is more informative than a mechanical percentage.

A lighter thirty-minute routine

  1. Read a meaningful chunk first: three pages is still fine, but four to six is also fine when the story is flowing. Do not stop merely because the old schedule says to stop.
  2. Protect the mental movie: let a sentence or short page finish before discussing it. Supply a quick English meaning only when an unknown word blocks the event.
  3. Check gist, not every line: after two or three pages, ask for a brief English retell, a point to the relevant picture, or one easy printed Filipino question. Skip the check when comprehension is already obvious.
  4. Sample exact translation: translate one interesting line, or investigate a line when RJ’s mental picture differs from the story. There is no need to prove every sentence.
  5. Let rereading become reading: familiar pages can be read straight through. The card game is optional; use one or two cards when it feels playful and put the deck away when it feels like testing.

A useful working balance is roughly 80–90 percent story and 10–20 percent deliberate noticing, with no need to time it precisely. School-exam days can simply be missed. The module does not have to occupy exactly four consecutive days.

English translation

Page 1

One morning, Polychrome brought a small book to the garden. She herself drew the pictures for Ozma.

Page 2

She sat with Dorothy, Toto, and Tin Woodman. The yellow bird perched on the table. “There are still three pages,” said Polychrome.

Page 3

Suddenly a strong wind blew. The three pages flew from the table. “Let’s find them before they are ruined!” Dorothy shouted.

Page 4

The first page went under the long bench. Toto sniffed and crawled beneath it. Dorothy pulled out the page.

Page 5

The bird saw the second page on a high rose branch. The bird pointed it out to Tin Woodman. He carefully took it.

Page 6

The third page was flying toward the little pond. Polychrome ran and caught it before it fell into the water. “Safe!” she said.

Page 7

They returned to the table, but the pages were separate. Each one had a picture of Toto and the blue ball. “Which is first?”

Page 8

Tin Woodman held the picture in which Toto was looking at the ball. “This is first. The ball is not rolling yet,” he said.

Page 9

Dorothy showed the picture in which Toto was chasing the ball rolling along the path. “This comes next,” she said. Toto barked happily.

Page 10

Polychrome raised the picture in which Toto was returning the ball. “And this is last.” They arranged the three pages in the correct order.

Page 11

Polychrome tied the pages into the book with a green ribbon. Ozma arrived. “We have a gift,” Dorothy said.

Page 12

They took turns reading the book to Ozma. She was delighted. Toto looked at his own picture, barked, and turned around happily.

Short answers to the six end questions

  1. Tatlong pahina. (Page 3)
  2. Sa ilalim ng upuan, sa sanga ng rosas, at malapit sa lawa. (Pages 4–6)
  3. Nakita ni Toto ang bola, hinabol niya ito, at ibinalik niya ito. (Pages 8–10)
  4. Hindi pa gumugulong ang bola sa unang larawan. (Page 8)
  5. Luntiang laso. (Page 11)
  6. Kay Ozma. (Page 12)

What to observe before Module 16

Continue with informal observations rather than a score:

  • Does the ordinary search-and-sequence story feel easier and more satisfying after the demanding science discussion?
  • Can RJ retell where each page was found?
  • Can she order the three ball pictures from either text or art?
  • Does she understand lumilipad, gumugulong, hinahabol, and ibinabalik from their visible actions?
  • When the first reading is allowed to flow, does her pace, expression, or enjoyment change?
  • How many new pages feel comfortable when three is treated as a starting point rather than a rule?
  • Does one gist check after two or three pages reveal enough comprehension without line-by-line translation?
  • When exact translation is sampled on only one line, does it reveal anything the gist check missed?
  • Do the optional Filipino questions still prompt spontaneous short answers when used sparingly?
  • Does the card review remain fun when it is offered rather than expected?
  • What practical unfamiliar-word count results, including any older-but-unsettled forms?
  • Do all three page locations and all three ball actions remain clear in the pictures?
  • Which harmless visual differences reward close reading, and which illustration becomes her favorite?
  • Do the stress support, flexible rhythm, thirty-minute ceiling, and enjoyment remain strong?

A useful short report: note story comprehension; the three search locations and three-picture order; contextual action forms; response to uninterrupted chunk reading; comfortable pages per session; usefulness of a single gist check and occasional translation sample; Filipino answers; practical unfamiliar count; optional card review; text-art clarity and productive discrepancies; pronunciation; rhythm; and enjoyment.

Production note

This is an original educational story inspired by the public-domain literary world of Oz. The yellow bird in the red floppy hat is an original recurring friend created for this project. The prose, questions, and original watercolor-and-colored-pencil illustrations were created for this reader and avoid film-specific likenesses and embedded text.
