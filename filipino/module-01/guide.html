Module 1 · Adult material

Shared-reading guide

Dorothy at ang Munting Binhi · Translation, vocabulary, grammar, questions, and a four-day reading plan.

Purpose: Module 1 consolidates the successful level of the calibration story. It is not a test and not a deliberate jump in difficulty. Its new language is meant to become understandable through sequence, repetition, and pictures.

Open child reader Print this guide

How Module 1 was calibrated

Module 0 worked as intended. RJ understood the plot in detail after one or two readings, retained attention and enjoyment, recognized repeated words in new places, understood the location phrases, inferred verb forms from context, and handled the Filipino questions. Module 1 therefore holds the level steady.
  • Same scale: 12 pages, 260 running Filipino words (Module 0 had 253), and 7 illustrations.
  • Same reading rhythm: three new pages per day, preceded by rereading all earlier pages.
  • Familiar anchors: the same four characters, the garden, colors, locations, hanap, tulong, biglang, and short dialogue.
  • New but inferable actions: finding a seed, comparing locations, digging, planting, watering, waiting, and growing.
  • No English in the child reader: translation and explicit grammar remain here so that the story can work naturally.

Alternating lines remains a good option, especially because RJ listens critically and can supply a natural pronunciation model. There is no need to settle on one reading method; echo reading, reading together, and alternating can serve different pages or different moods.

Suggested four-day sequence

DayReadingWhat the pictures and repetition support
1Preview the six words; read pages 1–3.Find the seed, understand that it can become a plant, and reject the first location because it is dark.
2Reread pages 1–3; add pages 4–6.Compare hard, dry ground with soft soil near light and water. Let RJ predict which place they will choose.
3Reread pages 1–6; add pages 7–9.Follow the planting sequence. Character roles make these pages suitable for alternating lines.
4Reread pages 1–9; finish pages 10–12.Notice the repeated waiting, the first sprout, and the later flower. Ask only as many questions as remain enjoyable.
LaterReread the whole story, switch roles, or let RJ narrate from the pictures.Look for anticipation, phrasing, and recognition of related verb forms—not a memorized performance.

6“We will plant the seed here,” Dorothy said. “Right,” Tik-Tok said. Toto barked. Toto was happy too.
7Tik-Tok dug a small hole. Dorothy carefully placed the seed inside. She covered it with soil.
8Dorothy carried a pitcher of water. Tik-Tok placed three stones around the spot. “So that we can see the place,” he said.
9Dorothy slowly watered the soil. “Water for the seed,” she said. “And patience from us,” said the bird.
10They returned the next day. There was no plant yet. They returned the following day. Still nothing. They waited and cared for the seed.
11One morning, Toto suddenly barked. There was a small green plant in the middle of the stones! “The seed sprouted!” Dorothy said.
12Many days passed. The plant grew and produced a large yellow flower. They were all happy. There were new seeds carried by the wind.

Grammar that matters in this story

1. The story shows aspect through a sequence, not a conjugation exercise

Filipino verbs commonly describe whether an action is contemplated, ongoing, or completed rather than simply matching an English tense. The pictures and plot establish the sequence: itatanim (“will plant”) comes before inilagay and tinakpan (“placed” and “covered”), followed later by tumubo (“sprouted”). Recognition from context is the intended learning mechanism.

2. Related-looking forms invite useful pattern recognition

The story includes several completed actions with um or nag: umulan, huminto, lumaki, lumipas, tumubo, naglakad, naghukay, naglagay, naghintay. Do not imply that one prefix has one fixed English translation. It is enough for RJ to notice that the beginning or inside of a root can change when the word becomes a verb.

3. Actor focus and object focus change what the sentence centers

Compare Naglagay si Tik-Tok ng tatlong bato, which centers Tik-Tok as the actor, with Inilagay ni Dorothy ang binhi sa loob, which centers the seed being placed. The markers change with that choice: si Tik-Tok in the first pattern and ni Dorothy in the second. Understanding the event matters more than naming the focus system.

4. Natin and atin include the listener

Itatanim natin ito means “we—including you—will plant this.” Tiyaga mula sa atin means “patience from us—including you.” This inclusive form suits the cooperative theme of the story.

5. Location language now supports comparison and reasoning

The familiar frames sa ilalim and sa tabi are reused, but now they help compare conditions: dark under the tree, hard and dry beside the path, soft and bright beside the river. If RJ can explain why one location is better, she is integrating the words rather than merely identifying them.

6. Linkers continue to appear naturally

Kayumangging binhi, malaking puno, magandang lugar, mabuting lupa, berdeng halaman, and bagong binhi attach -ng to a describing word. Maliit na ilog and maliit na binhi use the separate linker na.

What to observe before Module 2

Informal observations remain more useful than a score:

  • Can RJ explain why the first two planting places were rejected and why the third was chosen?
  • Does she connect itatanim with the later planting actions and tumubo with the sprout?
  • Which page or repeated frame becomes predictable first?
  • Can she retell the planting sequence from the illustrations, in either language?
  • Do the two “why” questions feel satisfying, or do they interrupt the story?
  • Does the same three-new-pages-plus-rereading rhythm still hold attention?
  • Which illustration becomes the favorite, and what detail does she notice in it?
A useful short report: note whether the difficulty still felt right, about how many genuinely new words appeared, whether the planting sequence and reasons were understood, which inflected forms transferred, and whether interest remained high through the rereads.
