For the adult reading partner

Dorothy at ang Tatlong Piraso ng Yelo

Use only as much support as helps. The story, pictures, predictions, and rereading should do most of the teaching.

Why this module stays at the same level

Module 13 again produced strong comprehension, Filipino answers to most page questions, and sustained enjoyment. The practical unfamiliar count fell to about 25 even with question words included. That success is evidence to preserve the level, not raise it. This module therefore keeps 12 pages, exactly 265 running story words, seven illustrations, four questions per page, and one compact experiment whose comparisons can be seen.

RJ’s palace-in-the-background objection was logically decisive: if the palace is visible, Button-Bright is not truly lost. Here, no background detail supplies an answer before the characters discover it. Harmless variations can still reward close reading, but an illustration should not contradict an essential cause, condition, or solution.

The science in plain language

The first pair changes one useful condition: one uncovered piece is in direct sun and one uncovered piece is in shade. The second pair is more controlled: both pieces are in the same shade, but one is wrapped in thick dry cloth. The cloth does not create cold. It slows heat transfer into the ice, so the wrapped piece melts more slowly. This is the same basic function as insulation; the U.S. Department of Energy explains that common insulation materials work by slowing conductive and convective heat flow.

This is a story experiment, not a laboratory measurement. Equal starting pieces, simultaneous starts, neighboring shaded positions, equal waiting time, and not moving the plates make the conclusion fair enough for Grade 3. If you try it at home, use a towel to catch water and never ask RJ to handle unusually cold material for long.

About the textbook’s verb labels

The photographed page is teaching a real and useful school category: aspekto ng pandiwa. The safest response is to connect its labels to meaning without turning them into an inflexible English-tense rule. Current DepEd material labels the three familiar categories Perpektibo (Naganap), Imperpektibo (Nagaganap), and Kontemplatibo (Magaganap). The older KWF Balarila ng Wikang Pambansa also records the traditional time-like terms pangnagdaan, pangkasalukuyan, and panghinaharap.

The textbook’s own sentence Nagtatanim ang magsasaka taon-taon is a helpful clue: its “pangkasalukuyan” form describes a habitual action, not necessarily something occurring at this second. In Module 14, keep the explanation concrete:

It is fine to name the category if RJ mentions school. There is still no need to make her recite a conjugation table. Also avoid the shortcut “um means past”: different Filipino verb families mark aspect in different ways. The ice sequence supplies a better rule—look at the whole form and the event in context.

Using the four-suit review game

The family’s variation is excellent retrieval practice after a story has first been learned in order. A simple fixed key prevents the adult from having to invent a question: hearts = question 1, diamonds = question 2, clubs = question 3, spades = question 4. Ace through ten select pages 1–10, jack selects page 11, and queen selects page 12; kings can mean “choose any familiar page.” Let RJ look at a neighboring page whenever the missing context becomes frustrating rather than fun.

End-of-story answer bank

1. Paano patas ang simula?

Shortest: Pareho at sabay.

Model: Magkakapareho ang yelo at sabay nilang sinimulan ang pagsubok.

Evidence: page 3. Choice: Sabay ba o magkaiba ang simula?

2. Ano ang ipinakita ng araw at lilim?

Shortest: Mas mabilis sa araw.

Model: Mas mabilis natunaw ang yelo sa araw kaysa sa lilim.

Evidence: pages 4, 7, 10. Choice: Saan mas mabilis—sa araw o lilim?

3. Bakit nasa lilim ang nakabalot?

Shortest: Para maihambing sa ikalawa.

Model: Parehong nasa lilim ang ikalawa at ikatlo upang maihambing ang may tela at walang tela.

Evidence: pages 4–5. Choice: Pareho ba o magkaiba ang kanilang lugar?

4. Ano ang tatlong resulta?

Shortest: Wala, maliit, pinakamalaki.

Model: Natunaw ang nasa araw, lumiit ang nasa lilim, at pinakamalaki pa ang nakabalot.

Evidence: page 10. Choice: Alin ang pinakamalaki—may tela o walang tela?

5. Ano ang ginagawa ng tela?

Shortest: Pinabagal ang init.

Model: Hindi gumagawa ng lamig ang tela; pinabagal nito ang pagpasok ng init.

Evidence: page 11. Choice: Gumagawa ba ito ng lamig o nagpapabagal ng init?

6. Ano ang sinabi bago, habang, at pagkatapos?

Shortest: Matutunaw, natutunaw, natunaw.

Model: Matutunaw ang yelo; natutunaw ito habang hinihintay; natunaw na ang nasa araw.

Evidence: pages 2, 6, 10, 12. Choice: Tapos na ba kapag natunaw na?

English translation

Page 1

One afternoon, Dorothy, Toto, and Button-Bright were in the garden. Professor Wogglebug arrived with the yellow bird. He was carrying three identical pieces of ice.

Page 2

“Ozma will arrive,” said the professor. “We need to keep the water cold.” Dorothy looked. “But these will melt!”

Page 3

They put one piece on each of three small plates. The ice pieces were the same size. They began the test at the same time.

Page 4

Dorothy put the first plate in the sun. Button-Bright put the second in the shade. Both were uncovered.

Page 5

The professor wrapped the third piece in thick cloth. He put it in the shade beside the second one.

Page 6

After a few minutes, they looked at the first two. “Has it melted?” asked the bird. “Not yet, but it is melting,” Dorothy answered.

Page 7

There was a lot of water on the plate in the sun. There was little water on the plate in the shade. “It melts faster in the sun,” said Button-Bright.

Page 8

The professor carefully opened the cloth. The wrapped ice was still almost whole. There was a little water under it. He wrapped it again.

Page 9

They waited the same length of time. They did not move the plates. Toto watched the cloth while the bird sang.

Page 10

When they looked again, the ice in the sun had melted. The ice in the shade was smaller. The wrapped ice was still the largest.

Page 11

“The cloth does not make cold,” the professor explained. “It slowed the entry of heat.” They used the ice to cool the pitcher.

Page 12

Ozma arrived. The water was cold. “The ice will melt. It is melting now. The one in the sun melted first,” Button-Bright explained. Toto barked, and the bird sang.

Capital letters mark the strongest syllable; the nephew’s fluent recording remains the best model for rhythm. Dictionary stress confirms lílim, platíto, balót, and the melted-state sense tunáw.

Let the changing ice carry the forms

Point, predict, and wait: matutunaw before the change, natutunaw while water gathers, and natunaw once the sunny piece is gone. If RJ supplies only one of the three, the picture and timeline are enough correction; terminology can remain secondary.

What to observe before Module 15

Continue with informal observations rather than a score:

  • Can RJ explain why the three ice pieces must begin the same size and at the same time?
  • Can she separately explain what the sun-versus-shade pair and the uncovered-versus-wrapped pair compare?
  • Does she understand why the wrapped ice and uncovered comparison ice are placed together in the same shade?
  • Can she predict and then retell the order of the three results?
  • Does she understand that the cloth does not make cold but slows heat entering?
  • Does she connect matutunaw, natutunaw, and natunaw to before, during, and completed melting?
  • Do the textbook labels help when attached to this visible sequence, or do they add confusion?
  • Does she avoid overgeneralizing that one piece of morphology always means “past” or “future”?
  • Do easy sino, static nasaan, and concrete ano questions still lead naturally into harder comparison questions?
  • Can she use a quoted page phrase to answer one of the science questions?
  • What practical unfamiliar-word count results when the Filipino page questions are included?
  • Does the combined vocabulary-and-stress table remain useful?
  • Does the four-suit review game remain enjoyable, and is the fixed suit-to-question key convenient?
  • Are all three positions, equal starting pieces, water amounts, and final sizes clear in the pictures?
  • Does any harmless visual difference reward close reading, and does any difference obscure the experiment?
  • Do the four-day rhythm, thirty-minute limit, pronunciation support, and enjoyment remain strong, and which illustration becomes her favorite?

A useful short report: note the practical unfamiliar count including questions; understanding of both controlled comparisons and the three results; understanding of insulation; transfer of the three tunaw forms; response to the school labels; Filipino answers; combined stress support; four-suit review; text-art science clarity and productive discrepancies; rhythm; and enjoyment.
