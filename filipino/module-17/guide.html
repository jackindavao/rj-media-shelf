For the adult reading partner

Dorothy at ang Tatlong Pahiwatig

A concrete three-clue search with one harmless false lead, familiar characters, and a final gift that celebrates RJ’s growing desire to read independently.

What Module 16 established

Comprehension was complete. RJ understood the parade order, both problems, both solutions, and the paired verb forms from context. Uninterrupted three-page reading again produced a detailed retell; an occasional translation sample found only one small verb misunderstanding, easily corrected.

Three pages remains her own preferred opening because she wants to reach the card game. She can then read enough selected older pages to bring the session total to roughly fifteen or twenty pages in thirty minutes. Skipping around is not weakening context: she remembers the stories in substantial detail. The fifth session also makes the end questions more approachable.

Most encouragingly, the offline copy led RJ to read on her own outside the shared session. Keep that independent reading free of required questions or reports. It is an additional pleasure-reading mode, not homework.

The current routine

  1. Shared sessions 1–4: read three new pages without interruption, then invite one brief retell. Correct only a misunderstanding that changes the event.
  2. Card review: continue while it feels like a game. Fifteen to twenty total pages is welcome when RJ chooses that amount; it is not a new quota.
  3. One question per card: the suit chooses among the four printed questions. Quoting the page is a legitimate bridge toward independently composed Filipino.
  4. Session 5: use the end questions, reconstruct the clue sequence, or reread favorite scenes.
  5. Independent tablet reading: do not test it afterward. A spontaneous comment, favorite picture, or question is enough.

English translation

Page 1

One morning, Dorothy found a white envelope on the table. It said: “For Dorothy, Toto, Button-Bright, and the yellow bird.”

Page 2

Button-Bright opened the envelope. “I am preparing a gift,” Tin Woodman wrote. “Find and follow the three clues.”

Page 3

The first clue was inside: “Find the place where the water sings.” “The fountain!” said Dorothy. Toto ran there.

Page 4

Behind the fountain, the bird saw the second envelope. It was hanging on a low branch. Button-Bright took it.

Page 5

“Follow the stones that sparkle,” he read. Two paths were in front of them. One had white stones; the other had red bricks.

Page 6

They followed the white stones. The path curved around and ended at a tree with a large hollow. Toto peered inside.

Page 7

The third clue was inside the tree: “Listen for a gentle tok-tok. There you will find the gift.” Everyone became quiet.

Page 8

They heard a tok-tok from above. “Could that be it?” Button-Bright asked. They looked up, but it was only a green bird pecking the tree.

Page 9

After a while, they heard a slow, steady tok-tok from the little workshop. “There!” said Dorothy. They followed the sound.

Page 10

Inside, Tin Woodman was tapping the wheel with a hammer. A small green cart was beside him. “You found me!” he said.

Page 11

“This is the book cart for the garden,” he explained. They helped put in the books. Then they pulled the cart toward the shade.

Page 12

Ozma was waiting under the tree. She chose a book, and everyone sat on the grass. From then on, there were stories to read in the garden.

Short answers to the six end questions

  1. Ang tatlong pahiwatig. (Page 2)
  2. Sa bukal, kung saan umaawit ang tubig. (Page 3)
  3. Sinundan nila ang landas na may mga batong kumikislap. (Pages 5–6)
  4. Ibon lamang iyon na tumutuka sa puno; wala roon ang handog. (Page 8)
  5. Ang mabagal at pantay na tok-tok mula sa pagawaan. (Page 9)
  6. Kariton iyon ng mga aklat. Nilagyan nila ito ng mga aklat at hinila sa lilim para makapagbasa sa hardin. (Pages 10–12)
