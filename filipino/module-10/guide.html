For the adult reading partner

Dorothy at si Propesor Wogglebug sa Dula ng mga Anino

This guide lets a non-Filipino-speaking adult support the story accurately without turning shared reading into a grammar lesson or a translation test.

Calibration decision

Module 9 again produced complete causal understanding, useful short Filipino answers, and high enjoyment. The practical count of 42 included transparent or familiar items, but it is a reason not to increase the load. Module 10 therefore stays at 12 pages and 265 words, returns to a tight visual experiment, and has 27 programmatically new surface forms. Four questions per page remain a menu of choices, not an assignment.

About dikit and ordinary touching

Your instinct was right. Dikit normally carries the idea of sticking, adhering, being attached, or being pressed closely against something. That made it exactly right for the iron key sticking to a magnet in Module 8, but too broad for the Module 9 string merely brushing a branch. Sayad is the more precise family there: sumasayad means “is brushing/touching against,” and sumayad means “brushed/touched against.” Module 9 has been corrected throughout without changing its plot.

Why this experiment fits

A shadow appears when an opaque object blocks light. Its apparent size changes with position: in this setup, moving the paper bird toward the lantern makes its shadow larger, while moving it away from the lantern and toward the screen makes the shadow smaller. The cloth also turns the experiment into a satisfying performance for Ozma.

Adult background references: the Exploratorium Light and Shadow starter cards and Science World's Light and Shadows activity. The story keeps only the relationship RJ can see directly.

Story map

  1. Pages 1–3: Wogglebug brings shadow-play materials; the first position produces no shadow.
  2. Pages 4–6: The object moves between light and screen; nearer and farther positions change the shadow's size.
  3. Pages 7–8: Toto and the professor discover playful new silhouettes.
  4. Pages 9–11: Ozma watches the finished bird-and-tree shadow play.
  5. Page 12: Ozma states the two visible rules.

Keep the session near thirty minutes

Your time limit is sensible. There is no benefit in completing every possible reread after attention would otherwise fade. Give priority to the three new pages and the current module's cumulative reread. Treat older stories as a rotating library: choose one whole favorite when time permits, or read only a familiar scene. Fluent pleasure reading and vocabulary retention matter more than finishing a quota.

RJ's preference for a meaning check after two or three familiar lines is also good evidence. Ask first for the overall picture. If a detail disappears, use one printed page question about that detail rather than returning permanently to line-by-line translation. Receptive understanding normally runs ahead of spontaneous sentence production; her short Filipino answers are genuine productive language and need not be expanded on demand.

Using the page questions

Choose one after a page and save the others for later readings. One word or a short phrase is a successful answer. If RJ answers in English, accept it as comprehension and read the short Filipino model once. If meaning differs, reread the relevant line, offer the simplest either/or choice, then move on.

End-of-story answer bank

1. Bakit walang anino?

Shortest: Nasa tabi ang ibon.

Model: Walang anino dahil wala sa pagitan ng ilaw at tela ang ibon.

Evidence: pages 3–4. Choice: Nasa tabi, o nasa pagitan?

2. Saan dapat ang bagay?

Shortest: Sa pagitan.

Model: Dapat nasa pagitan ng ilaw at tela ang bagay.

Evidence: page 4. Choice: Sa pagitan, o sa likod ng ilawan?

3. Ano ang nangyari nang ilapit?

Shortest: Lumaki ang anino.

Model: Nang inilapit ang ibon sa ilawan, lumaki ang anino.

Evidence: page 6. Choice: Lumaki o lumiit?

4. Ano ang nangyari nang ilayo?

Shortest: Lumiit ang anino.

Model: Nang inilayo ang ibon sa ilawan, lumiit ang anino.

Evidence: page 6. Choice: Lumiit o lumaki?

5. Paano gumawa ng puno ang propesor?

Shortest: Gamit ang apat na kamay.

Model: Ginawa niyang mga sanga ang apat niyang kamay.

Evidence: pages 8 and 11. Choice: Mga kamay o mga paa?

6. Ano ang dalawang natutuhan ni Ozma?

Shortest: Hinaharangan ang ilaw; lumalaki kapag inilalapit.

Model: Lumilitaw ang anino kapag hinaharangan ng bagay ang ilaw, at lumalaki ito kapag inilalapit sa ilawan.

Evidence: page 12. Choice: Hinaharangan ba ang ilaw? Mas malaki ba kapag mas malapit?

English translation

Page 1

That evening, Professor Wogglebug returned. He was carrying a lantern, white cloth, and a bird cut from thick paper.

Page 2

“We will prepare a shadow play for Ozma,” he said. Dorothy and Polychrome hung the white cloth between two trees.

Page 3

Dorothy placed the lantern several steps from the cloth. The professor held the paper bird beside the lantern. There was no shadow on the white cloth.

Page 4

“The object must be between the light and the cloth,” he explained. He moved the bird to the right place. Its little shadow suddenly appeared.

Page 5

The shadow was small. “How will we make it larger?” Dorothy asked. “We will move the bird closer to the lantern,” the professor answered.

Page 6

Polychrome moved the bird closer to the lantern. The shadow on the cloth grew larger. She moved the bird away from the lantern. The shadow grew smaller again.

Page 7

Toto suddenly passed between the lantern and the cloth. His large shadow appeared. He barked and jumped away.

Page 8

The professor raised his four hands. The large shadow moved like a strange tree. Dorothy and Polychrome laughed together.

Page 9

Ozma arrived and sat in front of the cloth. The friends went behind the cloth. The play was ready.

Page 10

Dorothy made the paper bird move. She brought it closer to the lantern, and the shadow grew. She moved it away, and the shadow shrank.

Page 11

The professor made his four hands into branches. The bird's shadow flew toward the tree. “A bird!” Ozma guessed happily.

Page 12

They bowed happily. “The object blocks the light, so a shadow appears. When the bird is moved closer to the lantern, the shadow grows,” Ozma said.

Let the sequence teach the forms

Ilalapit announces what they will do; inilapit reports that they did it. Lumaki and lumiit name opposite changes. The pictures and repeated test carry these meanings, so noticing is enough; no formal parsing is necessary.

Short answers are natural speech

Sa pagitan, sa ilawan, apat, wala, and lumaki ang anino are all complete conversational answers. They are a sound bridge between understanding and sentences RJ will eventually compose on her own.
