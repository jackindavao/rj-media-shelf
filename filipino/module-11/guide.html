For the adult reading partner

Dorothy at ang Bangkang Luwad

This guide lets a non-Filipino-speaking adult support the story accurately without turning shared reading into a grammar lesson or a translation test.

Calibration decision

Module 10 produced complete scientific and narrative understanding, useful Filipino answers, successful delayed meaning checks, and continued delight. The practical count of 34 includes older forms not yet fully secure, so it is evidence that retention is still developing—not a reason to raise the level. This module holds the scale at 12 pages and 265 words, uses 29 programmatically new surface forms, and keeps four optional questions per page.

Why the clay boat works

The amount of clay does not change, but its shape does. A compact clay ball displaces too little water before it is submerged and sinks. A broad hollow shape encloses open space and displaces more water, allowing the upward buoyant force to balance its weight. Adding cargo makes the boat sit lower. Once an edge dips beneath the surface and water enters, the total load increases rapidly and the boat sinks.

The child story deliberately stays with what RJ can observe: same clay, different shape, lower waterline with added stones, water entering at the rim, and a limited cargo. Adult background comes from the Smithsonian's Clay Boats activity and Science World's explanations of buoyancy and displacement and boat shape and open space.

Story map

  1. Pages 1–3: Wogglebug presents clay and stones; Dorothy predicts that the clay ball will sink.
  2. Pages 4–6: The ball sinks; the same clay becomes a broad hollow boat and floats.
  3. Pages 7–9: One and two stones ride safely; the third lowers the rim, lets water in, and sinks the boat.
  4. Pages 10–11: The friends widen the boat, raise its sides, limit the load, and replace the stones with Ozma's flower.
  5. Page 12: The flower reaches Ozma, and she names the two visible rules.

Thirty-minute reading rhythm

Keep doing what is working. Give the current three pages and current-module reread first claim on the session. An older story may be read whole, sampled, or skipped that day. On a familiar page, two or three lines before the meaning check is appropriate. Ask for the main picture first; if an important detail disappears, choose one targeted question from the table.

The easiest question families—sino, nasaan/saan, and concrete ano ang—are useful launch points because they elicit quick Filipino answers. A harder causal or sequence question can follow only when it feels conversational. “Coming close” in Filipino is productive work; the printed model supplies a correct form without demanding repetition.

End-of-story answer bank

1. Ano ang nangyari sa bilog na luwad?

Shortest: Lumubog.

Model: Lumubog ang bilog na luwad.

Evidence: pages 3–4. Choice: Lumutang o lumubog?

2. Paano binago ni Dorothy ang hugis?

Shortest: Pinatag at pinisil.

Model: Pinatag niya ang luwad, pinisil ang gitna, at tinaasan ang mga gilid.

Evidence: page 5. Choice: Ginawa ba niyang bangka o bola?

3. Ano ang nangyari sa isa at dalawang bato?

Shortest: Bumaba ngunit lumutang.

Model: Bumaba ang bangka ngunit lumutang pa rin.

Evidence: pages 7–8. Choice: Lumubog ba agad, o lumutang pa rin?

4. Bakit lumubog sa ikatlong bato?

Shortest: Pumasok ang tubig.

Model: Umabot ang tubig sa gilid at pumasok sa bangka.

Evidence: page 9. Choice: Pumasok ba ang tubig, o nanatiling tuyo?

5. Paano nila inayos ang bangka?

Shortest: Mas malapad at mas mataas.

Model: Ginawa nilang mas malapad ang bangka at mas mataas ang gilid.

Evidence: page 10. Choice: Mas mataas o mas mababa ang gilid?

6. Ano ang dalawang natutuhan nila?

Shortest: Mahalaga ang hugis at karga.

Model: Tumutulong ang hungkag na hugis, ngunit lumulubog ang bangka kapag sobra ang karga.

Evidence: pages 5–12. Choice: Mahalaga ba ang hugis? Puwede ba ang sobrang karga?

English translation

Page 1

The next morning, Dorothy and Toto went to the pond. Professor Wogglebug was waiting there with clay and three stones.

Page 2

“We will make the clay float,” said the professor. Dorothy held the round clay. It was heavy. Toto sniffed it.

Page 3

“Will it float or sink?” he asked. “It will sink,” Dorothy guessed. She carefully placed the round clay in the water.

Page 4

It sank immediately. Toto looked into the water and barked. The professor retrieved the clay with a spoon.

Page 5

“The same clay, a different shape,” he said. Dorothy flattened the clay. She pressed the middle and raised the sides. It became a little boat.

Page 6

She placed the boat flat on the water. It did not sink. The clay was floating! Toto jumped happily beside the pond.

Page 7

“Let us test the load,” said the professor. Dorothy put one white stone in the boat. It moved a little lower but still floated.

Page 8

She added a second stone. The boat was lower now. “It can still carry this,” Dorothy said. Toto watched quietly.

Page 9

She put in the third stone. The water reached the edge. Water entered, and the boat sank with the three stones.

Page 10

The professor retrieved the clay again. They made the boat wider and its edge higher. They put in only two stones.

Page 11

Ozma arrived with a yellow flower. She replaced the stones with the flower. Dorothy gently pushed the boat toward Ozma.

Page 12

The boat floated toward Ozma. Toto ran around the pond. “The hollow shape helped. But it will sink when the load is too great,” Ozma said.

Let prediction and result carry the forms

Lulutang and lulubog appear before the test. Lumutang and lumubog report what happened. Lumulutang names the result while everyone can still see it. The sequence is the explanation; no tense chart is needed.

Why short answers remain right

Si Toto, sa tubig, isang bato, lumubog, and ang hungkag na hugis are natural complete answers. Quick success with these patterns builds a reliable base for longer answers later.

What to observe before Module 12

Continue with informal observations rather than a score:

  • Can RJ explain why the clay ball sinks but the hollow boat floats even though the clay is the same?
  • Can she retell the shaping actions: flatten the clay, press the middle, and raise the sides?
  • Does she connect lulutang, lumutang, and lumulutang in context?
  • Does she connect lulubog with lumubog?
  • Can she describe what changes after the first, second, and third stones?
  • Does she understand that the third stone is not magically different—that the total load is now too great?
  • Can she explain why water entering over the rim makes the situation worse?
  • Which easy sino, saan, or concrete ano questions produce immediate Filipino answers?
  • Does one easier question help her approach a harder causal question in Filipino?
